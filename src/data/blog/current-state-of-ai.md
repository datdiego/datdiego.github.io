AI is changing quickly; it's one of the fastest growing and evolving technologies of our time. It's impossible to try and keep track of all the new stuff that comes out every day.

I keep myself updated through [TLDR](https://tldr.tech/), [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@diegoalducin8469), etc.

One of the most common topics that comes out every day is security.

Security of AI comes in many forms, but mainly it's about the risks it poses against other systems and people.

Anthropic just published today [Fable and Mythos 5.2](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2), state-of-the-art models for open-ended tasks. Fable has strong guardrails on cybersecurity and biology. But for mostly everybody, we don't need these huge models. I find Opus 5.5 at Medium quite capable of many things, including orchestrating agents.

Even Meta is using Anthropic as a reference, with them comparing their new model ([Ember](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-ember)) to Haiku, with lower costs.

However, Anthropic still seems to be in the lead in key AI research, with estimates achieving Recursive Self-Improvement by [2027](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-r-and-d-is-accelerating-towards).

Zvi claims, based on the METR reports, that most advances are due to AI doing AI R&D, and that there are no major bottlenecks. (I need to read the full report; it's quite long, but it's very immersive and detailed.) However, I think he is missing a key piece in all of this, which I often avoid as well: politics. We have yet to see how AI development evolves under this changing political environment.

One thing is for certain: people are starting to look more closely at how AI works and its risks. Which is why I intend to focus on the security of AI.
